# National Extreme Weather and Flood Reference Scenarios (March 2023)

Source: Israel Meteorological Service (IMS), Ministry of Transport[cite: 20]
Status: Official national reference scenario[cite: 20]
Note: Translated and adapted from the original Hebrew document[cite: 20].

## Flood Warning and Alert Lead Times

* **Initial Alert**: Issued approximately 5 days prior to an expected extreme weather event[cite: 20].
* **Joint Preliminary Alert**: The IMS and the Water Authority jointly issue a preliminary alert for river overflows at least 72 hours prior to the event, with continuous updates[cite: 20].
* **Focused Alert (Coastal and Northern Regions)**: Issued 24 to 48 hours prior to the event, detailing the start time, expected duration, and severity[cite: 20]. Precise pinpointing of the exact location of heavy rain cells on the coastal strip remains challenging[cite: 20].
* **Pinpoint Short-Term Alert (Southern and Eastern Desert Regions)**: Forecasting the exact location of intense desert storm cells is limited to very short notice, typically less than 1 hour[cite: 20].

## Region 1: Gush Dan and the Central Coastal Plain (Ayalon and Yarkon Streams)

* **Threshold**: Cumulative rainfall of 250–350 mm in the Gush Dan region over 2–5 days, with half of the amount falling in less than 24 hours[cite: 20]. 
* **Secondary Threshold**: Downpours exceeding 150–200 mm per day or 170–220 mm within 5–9 hours[cite: 20].
* **Expected Impacts**: 
  * Rapid rise and overflow of the Yarkon and Ayalon streams[cite: 20].
  * Severe blockages and inundation of the Ayalon Highway, Rokach Boulevard, and adjacent streets[cite: 20].
  * Large-scale flooding requiring mass evacuations in low-lying neighborhoods of Tel Aviv (Hatikva, Ezra, Montefiore, Nahalat Yitzhak), Jaffa, Bat Yam, and Holon[cite: 20].
  * Evacuations and rescues in Rosh HaAyin, Petah Tikva, and Hadar Yosef (Tel Aviv) due to Yarkon River and tributary overflows[cite: 20].
* **Accompanying Hazards**: Thunderstorms, heavy hail, strong winds, and "mini-tornadoes" causing property and agricultural damage[cite: 20].

## Region 2: Haifa Bay and Northern Coastal Plain (Kishon and Gdora Streams)

* **Threshold**: Cumulative rainfall exceeding 200 mm over 2–5 days on saturated ground, combined with initial bursts of 50–100 mm within a few hours in Haifa and the northern coastal plain[cite: 20].
* **Expected Impacts**: 
  * Overflow of the Kishon and Gdora streams[cite: 20].
  * Widespread flooding and required evacuations in the Zevulun Valley and the Krayot area (e.g., Kiryat Ata, Kiryat Bialik, Kiryat Nahum)[cite: 20].
  * Transportation and industrial shutdowns in the Haifa Bay industrial and commercial zones (e.g., Check Post, Kiryat HaPlada)[cite: 20].
  * Coastal streets in Bat Galim flooded due to high waves and seawater intrusion[cite: 20].
* **Operational Requirements**: Clearance of heavy mud and debris from intercity routes, and management of prolonged power outages caused by lightning strikes and fallen trees[cite: 20].

## Region 3: Southern and Eastern Israel (Desert Flash Floods)

* **Threshold**: Highly localized, extreme rain bursts, such as 50–75 mm within 30 to 60 minutes[cite: 20]. 
* **Expected Impacts and Evacuation Hotspots**: 
  * **Arava Highway (Highway 90)**: Susceptible to prolonged blockages and mass passenger entrapment between the flow channels of the Paran stream[cite: 20].
  * **Neot HaKikar and Ein Tamar**: Highly vulnerable to complete isolation for multiple days due to the overflow of the Tzin and Amatzia streams[cite: 20].
  * **Be'er Sheva**: The Be'er Sheva stream is prone to severe flooding, historically cutting off the Neve Noy neighborhood from the rest of the city[cite: 20].
  * **Eilat**: Extreme winds and flash floods in Wadi Taba can cause severe urban disruptions, destruction of marine and coastal infrastructure, and capsizing of vessels[cite: 20].
  * **Ben Gurion Airport (Natbag)**: Vulnerable to operational shutdowns due to severe sandstorms preceding the rain fronts[cite: 20].
* **Accompanying Hazards**: Lethal lightning strikes, heavy hail (4–5 cm diameter), and severe wind gusts capable of collapsing trees, agricultural greenhouses, and temporary structures[cite: 20].
