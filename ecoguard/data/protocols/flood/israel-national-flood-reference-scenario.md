# National Reference Scenario for Extreme Floods (2022-2026)

Source: NEMA (RAHEL), Meteorological Service, Water Authority, Ministry of Agriculture[cite: 16]
Status: Official national reference and planning document[cite: 16]
Note: Translated and adapted from the original Hebrew document[cite: 16].

## Flood Warning and Alert Lead Times

* **Initial Alert**: Issued 3 to 5 days prior to the expected event[cite: 16].
* **Focused Alert**: Issued 36 hours in advance, detailing the event's start time, expected duration, and severity[cite: 16].
* **Pinpoint Short-Term Alert**: 
  * Northern and Coastal areas: Issued 2 to 4 hours prior to the event[cite: 16].
  * Southern and Eastern areas (Desert/Arava): Issued 0.5 to 2 hours prior to the event[cite: 16].

## Hydrological Lag Times (Warning Buffers)

The lag time (the time between the accumulation of half the rainfall and the peak flow/discharge) indicates the available operational warning buffer before peak flooding hits[cite: 16]:
* **Yarkon Stream**: 20 hours[cite: 16].
* **Hadera Stream**: 13 hours[cite: 16].
* **Ayalon Stream**: 12 hours[cite: 16].
* **Gaaton, Kishon, Ekron, and Lakhish Streams**: 9 hours[cite: 16].
* **Desert Streams (Judean Desert and Negev)**: Highly rapid development, often less than 1 hour[cite: 16]. Operational response must rely primarily on rainfall forecasts rather than flow lag times[cite: 16].

## Regional Flood Thresholds and Expected Impacts

### Northern Region
* **Gaaton Stream (Nahariya)**: At a peak discharge of 15 m³/s, expect flooding of Highway 4, Highway 70, the Nahariya railway, Gaaton Blvd, and the Arena Mall[cite: 16].
* **Kishon Stream (Haifa Bay)**: At a peak discharge of 180 m³/s, expect flooding of Amakim Interchange (Highway 75), Megiddo airfield, the Check Post area, Haifa Refineries, and Lev HaMifratz railway station[cite: 16].

### Central Region
* **Ayalon Stream (Tel Aviv)**: At a peak discharge of 400 m³/s, expect flooding of the Ayalon Highway (from Moses-Arlozorov to Kibbutz Galuyot), railway tracks, and HaHagana and HaShalom train stations[cite: 16]. Flows exceeding 400 m³/s will inundate the Ezra neighborhood and southern Tel Aviv[cite: 16].
* **Yarkon Stream**: At a peak discharge of 220 m³/s, expect flooding of the Bnei Brak industrial zone, Yarkon Park, Bnei Brak train station, Red and Green Line light rail infrastructure, Kiryat Aryeh, and Ramat HaHayal[cite: 16].

### Southern and Eastern Regions
* **Lakhish Stream (Ashdod/Shfela)**: At a peak discharge of 230 m³/s, expect flooding of Hatzor Airbase, Ashdod train station, and the Re'em industrial area[cite: 16]. At 350 m³/s, expect flooding of the Ad Halom Interchange (Highway 4) and northern Ashdod[cite: 16].
* **Dead Sea Basin**: At a peak discharge of hundreds of m³/s, expect blockages and structural collapse along Highway 90, flooding of the Bar Yehuda airfield, Dead Sea Works pumping stations (P88, P89, Pool 5), and Ein Bokek hotels[cite: 16].
* **Negev and Arava**: At a peak discharge exceeding 500 m³/s, expect flooding and severe blockages on Highway 40 (Paran crossing) and Highway 90 (from Neot HaKikar to Paran)[cite: 16].
* **Eilat**: At a peak discharge of 500 m³/s, expect flooding of the area east of Highway 90 to the Red Sea, North Beach hotels, the Rabin border crossing (Jordan), and the Begin border crossing (Taba)[cite: 16].
