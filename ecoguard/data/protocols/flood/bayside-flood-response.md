# Bayside Flood Emergency Response Procedure

Source: Bayside Council & NSW State Emergency Service[cite: 11]
Status: Official operational procedure (Foreign Reference)[cite: 11]

## Applicability to Israel

This document is a foreign operational reference. It may support rescue
technique and responder-safety decisions, but it does not define legal
authority, agency responsibility, evacuation powers, or binding road-closure
thresholds in Israel. Israeli procedures take precedence.

## Flood Warning and Activation Criteria

* Flood response operations commence upon receipt of severe weather warnings, thunderstorm warnings (including heavy rain or storm surge), flood watches/warnings, flash flood warnings, or dam failure alerts[cite: 11].

## Evacuation Protocols

* Evacuations are initiated when homes or businesses are likely to flood, when remaining in isolated circumstances is unsuitable, or when essential energy/utility services are likely to fail[cite: 11].
* Evacuation decisions must be based on a combination of the following constraints[cite: 11]:
  * Duration of evacuation[cite: 11].
  * Characteristics of the community[cite: 11].
  * Numbers requiring evacuation[cite: 11].
  * Availability of evacuation routes and transport[cite: 11].
  * Time available for evacuation[cite: 11].
  * Evacuee management requirements[cite: 11].

## Flood Rescue Considerations

* Flood rescues should be performed only where a risk assessment indicates the risk to rescuers is acceptable[cite: 11].
* Animal welfare protocols during floods involve evacuation, emergency care, assessment, humane destruction, and disposal of affected animals[cite: 11].

## Post-Incident Return and Access Control

* Determinations on when communities can safely return are based on an assessment of: access and egress, communications, power supply, gas supply, infrastructure damage, hazardous materials, and public health risks (including sewerage)[cite: 11].
* Return access levels are specified in four phases[cite: 11]:
  1. Not suitable for access[cite: 11].
  2. Limited access by emergency services and response agencies[cite: 11].
  3. Limited access by residents and/or business operators[cite: 11].
  4. Full access[cite: 11].
