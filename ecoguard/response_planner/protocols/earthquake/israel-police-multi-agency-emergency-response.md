# Israel Police Multi-Agency Emergency Response Procedure

Source: Israel Police, procedure 221.109.01, *Master procedure for Israel
Police cooperation with emergency and rescue organizations at an incident*.
Published 29 July 2020 and marked valid until further notice in the source.

This is a concise English rendering of the operational responsibilities in the
official Hebrew procedure. It does not add dispatch quantities or earthquake
severity rules.

## Initial notification and response

When information about an incident is received, notification is distributed
among the control elements of the participating organizations with as much
detail as possible. Each organization dispatches its initial forces to address
the aspects within its lawful responsibility. Police at the scene performs an
initial situation assessment, coordinates the forces, reports upward, and calls
for additional forces when needed.

The procedure describes successive stages: notification and arrival, an initial
response in which each organization acts within its own responsibility, an
initial situation assessment, coordinated activity under Police command where
the procedure applies, and an orderly transfer or conclusion of responsibility.

## Life-saving and medical response

Life saving has priority. The procedure identifies rescue, treatment of
casualties, and evacuation as central joint efforts.

For initial medical care and evacuation, the combined activities include
first aid at the scene, triage, setting evacuation priorities, preparing
casualties for evacuation, and vehicle, air, or independent evacuation. Magen
David Adom leads this medical sub-effort with Israel Police assistance.

For locating and rescuing trapped people, the procedure identifies Israel
Police as the lead organization with professional assistance from Home Front
Command, Fire and Rescue, and Magen David Adom. The listed work includes locating
trapped people, rescue from vehicles and structures or rubble, evacuation for
medical response, and initial medical care.

## Scene control, access, and traffic

Israel Police leads scene isolation. Listed activities include blocking access
routes, establishing entry-control barriers, directing traffic outside and near
the scene so emergency and rescue forces can move freely, communicating closed
areas and movement restrictions, and planning alternative routes.

The local authority is identified as a participating organization in this
scene-isolation effort. Police is also responsible for keeping evacuation and
arrival routes open for response and medical evacuation vehicles.

## Fire and incident-source control

Fire and Rescue leads the sub-effort for dealing with incident-generating
hazards. The procedure includes locating and monitoring hazards, firefighting
and fire control, stopping hazardous-material releases, disconnecting
electricity, and other hazard-control actions where those hazards actually
exist. The local authority and other competent organizations may participate.

These are conditional capabilities. An earthquake detection or screening area
does not establish that fire, collapse, trapped people, casualties, blocked
roads, or hazardous-material releases exist.

## EcoGuard capability vocabulary

For EcoGuard planning output only, the official organizations correspond to
these existing unit-type identifiers:

- Israel Fire and Rescue: `fire_department`
- Israel Police: `police`
- Magen David Adom: `medical_services`
- Home Front Command: `home_front_command`
- local-authority emergency support: `municipal_emergency_team`

This normalization does not turn a recommendation into a dispatch and does not
specify how many stations, teams, crews, or vehicles are required.
