# Dead Sea Region Extreme Flood Reference Scenario

Source: Dead Sea Drainage Authority, Tamar Regional Council, and Emergency Bodies[cite: 18]
Status: Official regional reference and response plan[cite: 18]
Note: Translated and adapted from the original Hebrew document[cite: 18].

## Flood Severity and Alert Thresholds

The regional response is categorized into five severity levels based on flow duration and infrastructure impact[cite: 18]:
* **Level 1 (Blue) - Channel Flow**: Lasts a few hours with a warning time of up to 3 days[cite: 18]. No impact on infrastructure[cite: 18].
* **Level 2 (Green) - Flood Forecast**: Lasts hours to a full day, with a warning time of up to 3 days[cite: 18]. No impact on infrastructure[cite: 18].
* **Level 3 (Yellow) - Significant Flood**: Lasts 1 to 2 days, with a warning time of up to 5 days[cite: 18]. Causes minor infrastructure damage requiring breakdown maintenance[cite: 18].
* **Level 4 (Orange) - Severe Flood (Reference Scenario)**: Lasts a few days, with a warning time of up to a week[cite: 18]. Causes significant infrastructure damage, including the destruction of Highway 90 at stream crossings, heavy debris, and falling boulders[cite: 18]. Leads to region isolation, damage to critical utilities (power, communications, sewage), and high volume of rescue operations amidst a shortage of first responders[cite: 18].
* **Level 5 (Red) - Extreme Flood**: Lasts a week to several weeks, with a warning time of up to a week[cite: 18]. Causes most severe infrastructure destruction requiring complete reconstruction[cite: 18].

## Incident Command Sectors

To manage Command and Control (C2) challenges across varying administrative boundaries and multiple responding agencies, the Dead Sea region is divided into specific operational sectors based on hydrological characteristics and land use[cite: 18]:
1. **Megilot Sector**[cite: 18]
2. **Masada - Ein Gedi Sector**[cite: 18]
3. **Bokek Sector** (Includes the specific Hotel Sub-Sector)[cite: 18]
4. **Sodom Sector**[cite: 18]

## Operational Challenges and Guidelines

* **Isolation Management**: Incident commanders must maintain the "fabric of life" (medical services, education, and employment) within sub-regions that become entirely disconnected from one another due to floods[cite: 18].
* **Infrastructure Restoration**: Prioritize the rapid restoration of longitudinal traffic routes (e.g., Highway 90) and critical utility lines (water, sewage, treated wastewater, gas, and electricity) severely damaged by the floods[cite: 18].
* **Civilian Extraction and Crowd Control**: Prepare for the entrapment of hikers, tourists, and drivers in flash floods, and manage severe traffic jams and crowds caused by road blockages, heavy debris, or traffic accidents[cite: 18].
* **Reservoir Breaches**: Monitor and respond to secondary hazards such as reservoir overflows or breaches (e.g., Tzin Reservoir) and their subsequent downstream damage[cite: 18].
* **Resource Scarcity**: All response operations must account for extreme shortages in available resources and first responders during a Level 4 (Orange) event[cite: 18].
