# Armidale Regional Flood Emergency Response Procedure

Source: Armidale Regional Council & NSW State Emergency Service[cite: 12]
Status: Official operational procedure (Foreign Reference)[cite: 12]

## Applicability to Israel

This document is a foreign operational reference. It may support rescue
technique and responder-safety decisions, but it does not define legal
authority, agency responsibility, evacuation powers, or binding road-closure
thresholds in Israel. Israeli procedures take precedence.

## Flood Warning and Activation Criteria

* Flood response operations commence upon receipt of severe weather warnings, thunderstorm warnings (including heavy rain or storm surge), flood watches/warnings, flash flood warnings, or dam failure alerts[cite: 12].
* Flash flooding on tributary streams typically presents a rapid time of rise (around one hour) with a three-hour recession time[cite: 12].

## Dam Failure Alert Thresholds (Reference Model)

The following thresholds serve as an operational reference for escalating dam failure alerts based on rate of rise and water levels above Full Supply Level (FSL)[cite: 12]:
* **Protection Alert**: Triggered when the water level reaches 0.3m above the spillway crest[cite: 12].
* **White Alert (Prepare to Evacuate)**: Triggered when the rate of rise is < 0.8m/hr and storage level is >= FSL + 1m; OR rate of rise is >= 0.8m/hr and storage is >= FSL + 0.5m[cite: 12]. 
* **Orange Alert (Evacuation Order for highest risk)**: Triggered when the rate of rise is < 0.8m/hr and storage is >= FSL + 3.53m; OR rate of rise is >= 0.8m/hr and storage is >= FSL + 1m (but below FSL + 5.5m)[cite: 12].
* **Red Alert (Full Evacuation Order)**: Triggered when the rate of rise is < 0.8m/hr and storage reaches the Imminent Failure Level (IFL); OR rate of rise is >= 0.8m/hr and storage is >= FSL + 5m[cite: 12].

## Evacuation Protocols

* Evacuations are initiated when homes or businesses are likely to flood, when living in isolated circumstances becomes unsuited, or when essential energy/utility services are likely to fail[cite: 12].
* Evacuation decisions are based on[cite: 12]:
  * Duration of evacuation and length of isolation[cite: 12].
  * Characteristics of the community and numbers requiring evacuation[cite: 12].
  * Availability of evacuation routes and transport[cite: 12].
  * The ability for existing levees or flood protection works to fulfil their intended function[cite: 12].
  * Time available for evacuation and evacuee management requirements[cite: 12].
* Evacuation operations should be carried out before inundation occurs when there is evidence to suggest that creeks and tributaries will break their banks[cite: 12].

## Flood Rescue Considerations

* Flood rescues are performed only where training and equipment are suitable and a risk assessment indicates the risk to rescuers is acceptable[cite: 12].
* Animal welfare operations include evacuation, emergency care, assessment, humane destruction, and disposal of affected animals, as well as the supply of emergency fodder and water[cite: 12].

## Post-Incident Return and Access Control

* The determination of safe return is based on ongoing risks to public safety, specifically: access and egress, communications, power supply, gas supply, infrastructure damage, hazardous materials, and public health risks[cite: 12].
* Access levels are defined in four categories[cite: 12]:
  1. Not suitable for access[cite: 12].
  2. Limited access by emergency services and response agencies[cite: 12].
  3. Limited access by residents and/or business operators[cite: 12].
  4. Full access[cite: 12].
* A "Reduced Threat: Return with Caution" warning is issued when the immediate danger to life and property has passed[cite: 12].
