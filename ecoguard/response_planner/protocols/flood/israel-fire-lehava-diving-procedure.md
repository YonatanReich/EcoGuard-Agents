# Fire and Rescue Israel - Lehava Unit Diving Platoon Operation Procedure

Source: Fire and Rescue Israel, Lehava Unit & Operations Division[cite: 26]
Status: Official operational and safety procedure[cite: 26]

## Command, Control, and Safety Authority

* **Incident Commander (IC)**: The Unit Commander (or the first arriving officer until the unit commander assumes command) oversees the entire operational zone, determines the general search plan, and coordinates with external agencies (Police, ZAKA, IDF)[cite: 26].
* **Dive Mission Commander**: Appointed by the IC to supervise the specific diving mission, execute risk assessments, establish search patterns, and manage water/land logistics[cite: 26].
* **Safety Officer (DSO)**: Responsible for the safety of the wet dive team[cite: 26]. **Absolute Authority**: The Safety Officer holds the unchallengeable authority to halt any diver, stop a dive, or cancel an entire operation immediately without needing to give explanations on the spot[cite: 26]. The Safety Officer does not participate in the wet dive tasks[cite: 26].
* **Dive Team Leader (Level 3 Diver)**: Manages team synchronization, entry/exit safety, precise execution of search patterns, and evidence preservation underwater[cite: 26].

## Rapid Response vs. Search and Recovery Missions

### 1. Rapid Response / Rescue Task ("The Golden Hour")
* Defined as any mission where survivor prospects exist, meeting the "Golden Hour" criteria (under research parameters, successful resuscitations have occurred in cold water up to 60 minutes underwater)[cite: 26].
* **LSP (Last Seen Point) Search**: Conducted immediately following a fast situational assessment and short witness debrief[cite: 26]. In non-flowing water bodies, search in a radius around the LSP + 10%; in flowing water, apply patterns matching current speed and direction[cite: 26].
* **Relaxed Safety Parameters (Rescue Mode Only)**: During rapid rescue operations exclusively, safety parameters may be streamlined: diving in reduced teams (e.g., Tender diving with primary and backup divers), operating without a dedicated medical team on-site, and utilizing initial estimates for water contamination levels[cite: 26].

### 2. Drowning Victim Search and Recovery
* Applied when operations exceed the "Golden Hour"[cite: 26]. Requires strict risk management, formal crime-scene isolation, evidence handling protocols, and establishing a structured Search Command Post (חפ"ק)[cite: 26].

## Standard Underwater Search Methods

* **Circular / Compass Sweep (מחוגה)**: Ideal for rapid response (Rapid Response) around an LSP in flat, unobstructed terrain with visibility of at least 2–3 meters[cite: 26].
* **Parallel / Jackstay Pattern (מקבילים / Jackstay)**: Efficient for scanning medium-sized areas (up to 50 meters) in low visibility or black-water conditions using a guide line[cite: 26].
* **Sweep Line (יישור קו)**: A line of divers moving across a defined sector; effective in moderate-to-good visibility[cite: 26].
* **Tender Line / Dock Walking (צלילת Tender / הליכת מזח)**: Highly accurate method for black water or contaminated water environments where a surface tender guides and controls the diver via a tether line[cite: 26].

## Contaminated Water Protocols

* When operating in suspected contaminated or sewage-polluted water, operations must implement a strict three-zone system (Hot, Warm/Decon, Cold zones)[cite: 26].
* **Required Diver Protection**: Dry suit rated for contaminated water, dry gloves, a tight-fitting hood preventing water ingress, and a full-face mask[cite: 26].
* **Decontamination**: A dedicated wash station with water, soap, and degreasers must be fully operational prior to the first diver entering the water[cite: 26]. Post-dive gross decontamination (e.g., 3%–5% bleach solution rinse) is mandatory before removing equipment[cite: 26].

## Medical Support and Emergency Requirements

* All standard diving operations must ideally be accompanied by a qualified medic (or higher) equipped with a trauma backpack and an oxygen cylinder providing at least 90 minutes of supply[cite: 26].
* In case of a dive emergency (such as air depletion, entanglement, or equipment failure), divers must follow standardized touch-signal communications or execute pre-briefed emergency ascents[cite: 26].
