# Wildland Fire Safety Doctrine: Standard Firefighting Orders, Watch Out Situations, and LCES

Source: National Wildfire Coordinating Group (NWCG), Incident Response Pocket Guide
(IRPG), PMS 461, as published by the U.S. National Park Service and the NWCG.
Status: work of the U.S. federal government, public domain.

This document holds the core safety and engagement doctrine of U.S. wildland
firefighting. It governs whether and how responders may engage a fire, and it is the
authority to consult when deciding if an assignment is safe to accept.

## Purpose and Origin

The 10 Standard Firefighting Orders were developed in 1957 by a task force studying
ways to prevent firefighter injuries and fatalities. The task force drew on lessons from
fatal wildland fires including the Blackwater fire of 1937 and the Mann Gulch fire of
1949. Shortly afterwards the 18 Situations That Shout Watch Out were developed.

The two lists are used together and are referred to collectively as "the 10 and 18". If
firefighters follow the 10 Standard Firefighting Orders and are alerted to the 18 Watch
Out Situations, much of the risk of firefighting can be reduced. The Watch Out
Situations are more specific and cautionary than the Orders; they describe
circumstances that expand on the Orders.

The Standard Firefighting Orders are written to be followed in order. They are not a
menu. The tenth order is deliberately last: aggressive suppression is authorised only
after the preceding nine have been satisfied.

## The 10 Standard Firefighting Orders

Orders 1 through 3 concern fire behaviour:

1. Keep informed on fire weather conditions and forecasts.
2. Know what your fire is doing at all times.
3. Base all actions on current and expected behavior of the fire.

Orders 4 through 6 concern fireline safety:

4. Identify escape routes and safety zones and make them known.
5. Post lookouts when there is possible danger.
6. Be alert. Keep calm. Think clearly. Act decisively.

Orders 7 through 9 concern organizational control:

7. Maintain prompt communications with your forces, your supervisor, and adjoining forces.
8. Give clear instructions and ensure they are understood.
9. Maintain control of your forces at all times.

Order 10 is conditional on all of the above:

10. Fight fire aggressively, having provided for safety first.

## The 18 Watch Out Situations

Each of these is a circumstance that has historically preceded entrapments, injuries, or
fatalities. Encountering one does not by itself prohibit engagement, but it requires that
the hazard be mitigated and communicated before work continues. Multiple
simultaneous watch out situations are a strong indicator that the assignment should be
reconsidered.

1. Fire not scouted and sized up.
2. In country not seen in daylight.
3. Safety zones and escape routes not identified.
4. Unfamiliar with weather and local factors influencing fire behavior.
5. Uninformed on strategy, tactics, and hazards.
6. Instructions and assignments not clear.
7. No communication link with crew members or supervisor.
8. Constructing line without safe anchor point.
9. Building fireline downhill with fire below.
10. Attempting frontal assault on fire.
11. Unburned fuel between you and the fire.
12. Cannot see main fire, not in contact with someone who can.
13. On a hillside where rolling material can ignite fuel below.
14. Weather becoming hotter and drier.
15. Wind increases and/or changes direction.
16. Getting frequent spot fires across line.
17. Terrain and fuels make escape to safety zones difficult.
18. Taking a nap near the fireline.

## LCES: Lookouts, Communications, Escape Routes, Safety Zones

LCES is the framework wildland firefighters use to engage in fire suppression safely,
and it is described as the foundation of safe fire suppression action. The four elements
must be established and made known to every firefighter before they are needed, not
at the moment they become necessary.

- **Lookouts.** Post personnel who can see both the fire and the crew, and who can
  give warning in time for the crew to reach a safety zone. Post lookouts whenever
  there is possible danger, per Standard Firefighting Order 5.
- **Communications.** Maintain a working communication link with crew members,
  supervisors, and adjoining forces. Loss of the communication link is Watch Out
  Situation 7 and is grounds for disengaging.
- **Escape Routes.** Identify at least one, and preferably two, reliable routes from the
  work location to a safety zone. Escape routes must be timed against expected fire
  behaviour, not merely mapped, and they must be re-evaluated as the fire changes.
- **Safety Zones.** Identify areas where firefighters can survive without a fire shelter.
  Safety zone adequacy depends on flame length and on the size of the cleared area.

If any element of LCES cannot be established and maintained, the correct action is to
disengage and reposition rather than continue the assignment.

## Escape Route and Access Considerations

Standard wildland firefighting orders require that firefighters have at least one, and
preferably two, reliable escape routes at all times. Impeded or obstructed egress is the
principal factor jeopardising firefighter safety during structure defence operations.

Caution is required on access roads where a continuous fuel ladder runs from grass into
heavy fuel types, and where large accumulations of down-dead fuels run parallel to the
road. Flame lengths and thermal output in those conditions may exceed survivability
limits and may block egress.

## Operational Use in Assessment

When assessing a detected fire, this document supports the following judgements:

- Whether observed weather trends match Watch Out Situations 14 and 15 (weather
  becoming hotter and drier; wind increasing or changing direction), which elevate risk
  independently of current fire size.
- Whether terrain and fuel continuity make escape difficult (Watch Out Situation 17).
- Whether the fire can be seen and is in communication range (Watch Out Situation 12).
- Whether an aggressive suppression posture is justified at all, given that Standard
  Firefighting Order 10 makes it conditional on the first nine orders being satisfied.
