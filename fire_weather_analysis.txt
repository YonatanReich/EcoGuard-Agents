FIRE WEATHER DETECTOR - QUALITY ANALYSIS OF A REAL RUN
run at 2026-09-15, against the live database

Companion files:
  fire_weather_run.txt     the run itself, with full context per signal

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT PRODUCED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1,174 cells swept, 518 assessable, 2,072 rarity computations, 4 signals.
All four: relative_humidity_2m = 16%, rarity 1.0, in four adjacent cells
centred on 30.60 N 34.54 E - the central Negev highlands, near Sde Boker.

The coordinator folded them into one incident in the emergency queue.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IS THE STATISTIC CORRECT?  Yes. Verified three ways.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The baseline claims median humidity of 74% for that cell at 06:00 UTC in
September. That looked wrong for a desert, so it was checked against our own
stored observations for the same cell and hour:

    75, 84, 80, 83, 73, 81, 48, 48, 40, 38, 36, 16

Median 74. The baseline is right, and 16% is below the decade minimum of 17%.
The reading is genuinely the driest that cell has been at that hour in ten
years, and `beyond_record=True` correctly says the rarity is a floor.

An independent check agrees the area is dangerous: the CFFDRS fire weather
index stored for those exact cells reads FWI 23.9-25.0, danger_class "high".

So the claim "this is exceptionally dry for here" is true.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IS IT A FIRE CANDIDATE?  No. Three reasons, worst last.
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

REASON 1 - the rest of the hour disagrees.

    relative_humidity_2m     16.0 %      rarity 1.0000   <- the signal
    vapour_pressure_deficit   2.8 kPa    rarity 0.9578
    temperature_2m           25.7 C      rarity 0.8332
    wind_speed_10m            2.9 km/h   rarity 0.0662   <- 6th percentile

The wind is not merely unremarkable, it is anomalously CALM - the 6th
percentile. 25.7 C is mild. Fire needs fuel, ignition and spread; this hour
offers dry air and nothing else. One variable out of four is extreme and the
one that governs spread points the other way.

REASON 2 - sparse fuel.

NDVI in the four cells is 0.113 - 0.123, against a national median of 0.163
and p25 of 0.118. Bottom quartile: desert scrub. Not nothing, but not the
Carmel. Nothing in the detector looks at fuel at all, although the vegetation
collector has been storing NDVI per cell all along.

REASON 3 - and this is the real problem - THE DETECTOR IS BLIND WHEN IT
MATTERS AND LOUD WHEN IT DOES NOT.

Baselines are per hour of day. A reading is "rare" only against that same hour
on other days. Afternoons in the Negev are reliably hot and dry, so an extreme
afternoon is statistically ordinary. Dawns are reliably humid, so any dry dawn
is extreme.

The same 16% humidity, in the same cell, judged at each hour:

     UTC  local   RH median   rarity of 16%   reported?
       0     3h          90          1.0000   YES
       4     7h          92          1.0000   YES
       6     9h          74          1.0000   YES   <- today's signal
       8    11h          53          0.9846   no
      10    13h          43          0.9713   no
      12    15h          39          0.9691   no
      14    17h          46          0.9800   no
      16    19h          62          1.0000   YES
      20    23h          87          1.0000   YES

16% humidity at 3 a.m. is reported. The identical 16% humidity at 3 p.m. -
alongside 30 C and the day's peak wind - is not.

This is not theoretical. Replaying 14 days of stored weather, by hour:

     UTC local signals
       3    6h      90  ########################
       4    7h      61  ################
       9   12h       3                            <<< fire-danger window
      10   13h       6  #                         <<< fire-danger window
      11   14h      15  ####                      <<< fire-danger window
      12   15h      14  ###                       <<< fire-danger window
      13   16h      25  ######                    <<< fire-danger window
      14   17h      35  #########                 <<< fire-danger window
      18   21h      96  ##########################

    12:00-17:00 local, the fire-danger window : 98 of 975 signals (10%)
    six of twenty-four hours, so neutral      : 25%

The detector is under-weighted 2.5x in the window when fires actually start,
and during that window the only variable that fires at all is wind - humidity,
temperature and VPD are silent from 10:00 to 17:00 local.

Rarity is ANTI-CORRELATED with fire danger across the daily cycle. This is
exactly the failure `shared/signals.py` warns about in its own docstring -
"40 C in a Negev August is statistically dull and is what burns the country
down" - appearing inside the detector rather than downstream of it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SIGNAL RATE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

14-day replay: 975 signals in 477,608 assessments = 0.00204.
That is twice the 0.001 the 0.999 threshold implies, and very uneven:

    2026-09-12   0.00030      quiet
    2026-09-14   0.00356
    2026-09-15   0.01751      17x budget

The budget arithmetic in coordinator/README.md assumes independent cells.
Weather is spatially correlated, so a real dry spell lights dozens at once.
That is correct behaviour, not a bug, but "~28 signals a day" should be read
as "a handful on quiet days, a few hundred on event days".


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ONE HYPOTHESIS TESTED AND DISPROVED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Expected failure: a signal per variable means a windy but humid or rainy day
emits FIRE signals. Checked all 328 wind-triggered signals in the replay -
zero occurred at RH >= 60%, zero during rain. In September, anomalous wind
travels with hot dry air (typically 36 C at 21-26% RH).

The concern still stands for winter, when a gale is wet and cold, but the
stored history is all September and cannot show it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WOULD FIX IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a rewrite. The statistics are sound and the plumbing works; what is
missing is a floor on absolute conditions, so that "unusual" is only asked
where fire is physically possible.

  1. A minimum-conditions gate - no signal below roughly 25 C, or above
     roughly 40% humidity, whatever the rarity. Same category as the
     precipitation exclusion: refusing to ask a question whose answer cannot
     mean anything. Costs about five lines.

  2. A fuel gate on NDVI. The data is already collected per cell.

  3. Prefer FWI. The CFFDRS index is already stored per cell with a
     danger_class, is absolute rather than relative, and is the domain's own
     answer to "is this fire weather". It needs a baseline table before it can
     express rarity, but the absolute value is usable immediately.

Item 1 alone would have suppressed all four of today's signals, and would
leave the 15:00 wind signals - the ones that point at real fire weather -
untouched.
